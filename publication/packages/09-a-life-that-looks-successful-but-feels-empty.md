**Caption**

```
Almost 2,000 students in the Midwest and Southern California rated their lives. Everyone expected Californians to be happier. Californians liked their weather a lot more, but their overall life satisfaction was the same.

The researchers called it a focusing illusion: we give the visible, distinctive parts of a life far more weight than they have in the living of it. We do it to our own lives too.

What are you judging yours by?

Evidence: Schkade & Kahneman (1998). Full essay on the Substack, link in bio.
```

## 6. Three Substack Notes (aim 250–300 characters; embed the post, don't link out)

1. Everyone expected students in California to be happier than students in the Midwest. They liked their weather much more. Their overall life satisfaction was the same. We judge lives by their most visible features, and we often do it to ourselves. *(246 chars)*
2. Aristotle on money, written about 2,300 years ago: wealth is 'merely useful and for the sake of something else'. The empty successful life is often one organised around things that are for something else, with the something else left out. *(238 chars)*
3. Two lists. On the left, what your life looks like from outside. On the right, what a normal Tuesday evening actually holds. The gap between them is worth an hour of your attention this week. *(190 chars)*
