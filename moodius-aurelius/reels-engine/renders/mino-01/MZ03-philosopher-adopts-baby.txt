Epictetus had been enslaved, walked with a limp and never married. An ancient commentator, Simplicius, records that late in life, when a friend was going to abandon his baby, Epictetus took the child and brought it up.

We only have this from a writer centuries later, so treat it as testimony, not a birth certificate. It's still a good one.

Who took someone in when nobody else would?

Source: George Long's introduction to the Discourses (1877), relaying Simplicius.

— Moodius
