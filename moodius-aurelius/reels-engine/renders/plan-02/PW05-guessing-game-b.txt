Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 3.4 (tr. George Long), describing the person he wanted to be: one who is "not often, nor yet without great necessity and for the general interest, imagining what another says, or does, or thinks."

Not never. Not often. He knew the pull of it: the slow reply, the half-second face in the meeting, the like that didn't come. He also knew where that attention goes: into a head you can't see, away from the one thing you can actually act on.

Tonight, count today's guesses. Then ask the only question with an answer: what's mine to do here?

Send this to the friend who's overthinking a text right now.

How many guesses?

— Moodius
