In AD 64 Lyon burned to the ground in a single night. Seneca wrote to his friend Liberalis, who came from there, and his advice wasn't comfort. It was: nothing should be unexpected. Whatever has happened to anyone can happen to you, so think it through once, calmly, before it does. (Letters 91, in our words.)

That's the line between dread and protection. Dread rehearses the feeling and stops. Protection rehearses the event and ends in a plan: what you'd do first, who you'd call, what you'd still have.

Same thought. Different ending.

Send this to the friend who rehearses disasters in the shower.

Dread or plan?

— Moodius
