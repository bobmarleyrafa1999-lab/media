Epictetus, Enchiridion 48 (tr. George Long): the uninstructed person "never expects from himself profit nor harm, but from externals", while "the condition and characteristic of a philosopher is this: he expects all advantage and all harm from himself."

You can hear it in one sentence. "The train, then my manager, then the weather" has no one in it who can do anything. "I left late, I didn't push back, I forgot the coat" is the same day, and it's miserable too, but now it has you in it, and you're the only part that can change by tomorrow.

Not blame. Location.

Send this to the friend whose bad days are always somebody else's.

Who's in your sentence?

— Moodius
