Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 10.16 (tr. George Long): "No longer talk at all about the kind of man that a good man ought to be, but be such."

He wrote it to himself, in a notebook nobody was meant to read, after years of arguing the theory. The man whose lecture notes taught him Stoicism, Epictetus, had been a slave; his version of the same idea was that philosophy is shown in what you do, not what you can explain. (Today's carousel has the whole story.)

The piece you do today can be small. A promise kept. A sentence you'd been avoiding.

Send this to the friend who can describe the good life in detail.

Whose is right?

— Moodius
