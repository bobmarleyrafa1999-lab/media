Part 2 of 3. Epictetus, who had been enslaved for the first part of his life, told his students that the real question before any decision is what you'd sell yourself for. Discourses 1.2 (tr. George Long): "it is you who know yourself, how much you are worth to yourself, and at what price you sell yourself; for men sell themselves at various prices."

He isn't against saying yes. He's against not knowing the price. The meeting you sat through, the favour you'll resent by Thursday, the "sure, no problem" that cost you a Sunday: each one was a sale. Some are worth it. Know which.

Send this to the friend who says yes before the sentence is finished.

Name the price?

— Moodius
