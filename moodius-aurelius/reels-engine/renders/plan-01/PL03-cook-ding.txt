In the Zhuangzi, a cook carves an ox for his ruler so smoothly it sounds like music. Asked how, he says his knife has been in use for nineteen years and is still sharp, because he follows the spaces between the joints instead of hacking at the bone. The ruler replies that he has just learned how to nourish his life.

The skill that looks like ease is usually nineteen years of paying attention. (Moodius's reading of Zhuangzi, Book III, tr. James Legge.)
