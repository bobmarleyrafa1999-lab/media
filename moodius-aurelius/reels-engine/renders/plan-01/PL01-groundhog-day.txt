Groundhog Day (1993, dir. Harold Ramis) is a comedy about the oldest idea in ethics. Phil only changes once he stops using the repeated day for himself and starts using it for other people: lessons, small rescues, a boy who never says thanks.

Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1 (tr. W. D. Ross): “we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts.”
