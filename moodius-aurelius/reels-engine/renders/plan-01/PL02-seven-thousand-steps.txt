10,000 steps is a round number, not a finish line. A 2022 meta-analysis of 15 cohorts (about 47,000 adults) found the drop in risk of death levelled off around 6,000–8,000 steps a day for adults over 60 and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults. A 2025 review of 57 studies found about 7,000 steps a day was linked to a 47% lower risk of death than 2,000, close to the figure for 10,000.

These are observational studies: they show links, not proof that steps cause the benefit. If you're at 3,000, 5,000 is progress.
