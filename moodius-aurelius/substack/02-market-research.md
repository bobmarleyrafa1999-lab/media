## 2. What the field does (patterns)
1. **Named-author authority.** Nearly every strong publication in A–C is led by a credentialed person (professor, psychiatrist, author). Moodius is anonymous, so its authority has to come from **visible sourcing** instead. That's why evidence labels are the core of the strategy, not decoration.
2. **A "start here" map.** The Honest Broker publishes a map of its archive; Figs in Winter keeps an article index. New readers arriving from Notes need a front door. → Moodius ships a *Start Here* post with the first essay.
3. **Series and numbering.** Mind in Context numbers its issues; A Stoic Breviary reads one letter at a time. Series give returning readers something to collect. → Moodius series names (§1 strategy).
4. **The reframe headline is everywhere** in masculinity writing ("loneliness is actually emptiness", "performative loneliness"). It's the dominant move, so it's also crowded. → Moodius uses it sparingly and backs it with data.
5. **Jungian Substacks are practitioner-led and rarely cite research.** That's fine for analysts, but it leaves a gap for a careful explainer that separates Jung's ideas from what lab psychology has tested.
6. **Journaling Substacks are mostly prompt lists.** Few explain why journaling works, when it doesn't (rumination), or connect it to philosophy.
7. **Grief and mortality writing is personal-memoir-led.** An anonymous brand can't do memoir, so Moodius writes about grief through research (dual-process model, continuing bonds) and philosophy, and keeps the cadence low.

## 4. Underexplored questions with strong audience relevance
These are the gaps the backlog targets first. "Why open" is my reading of the field.
1. **Why does journaling sometimes make you feel worse?** (Expressive writing has small average effects; rumination is real.) Nobody in the journaling lane addresses it.
2. **What did Jung actually say about the shadow, and what has psychology tested?** A careful bridge between #24–25 and lab work on projection and suppression.
3. **Men who are "fine": self-reliance as the norm most tied to poor outcomes** (Wong et al. 2017). The masculinity lane argues about loneliness; few name *self-reliance* specifically.
4. **The arithmetic of friendship:** hours needed to make a friend (Hall 2019) versus how adult men spend time.
5. **Success that doesn't land inside:** hedonic adaptation, extrinsic goals and the 2023 Killingsworth–Kahneman–Mellers resolution on income and wellbeing.
6. **Stoicism without suppression:** what the Stoics meant by "first movements", and what emotion-regulation research says about suppression vs reappraisal.
7. **Approval-seeking at work:** shame vs guilt and why criticism feels like a verdict.
8. **Grief without a timetable:** oscillation and continuing bonds, written for men who "should be over it".

## 5. Headline techniques and publishing patterns
**From data (with caveats):** WriteStack's analysis of 27 million Substack Notes ([Günel & Orel, 19 Oct 2026](https://writebuildscale.substack.com/p/you-had-questions-about-notes-we)) reports that 77% of Note impressions come from the feed, the top 10% of Notes take 68% of impressions, Notes of 250–299 characters averaged more likes than average, outside links reduced reach (0.86× typical) while embedded Substack posts converted best to paid subscribers, and posting time barely matters (~5%). Caveats: correlational, no full methods, commercial interest. Substack itself has said its network drove about 40% of free subscriptions and recommendations about 20% ([On Substack, c. late 2022](https://on.substack.com/p/turn-on-your-growth-engine)); that figure is old.

**How Moodius applies it:** Notes are written at ~250–300 characters, embed the essay rather than linking out, go out daily on weekdays, and the publication sets up 3–5 recommendations with aligned newsletters once it has 4 essays live.

**Headline patterns (house rules, opinion):**
| Pattern | Example shape | Use |
|---|---|---|
| The precise paradox | "The Loneliness of Being Fine" | Flagship essays |
| The unexpected noun | "The Arithmetic of Friendship" | Research-led pieces |
| Old voice, new problem | "What Seneca Knew About Your Salary" | Philosophy pieces, sparingly |
| The honest limit | "Why Writing It Down Helps (A Little)" | Builds trust; fits evidence labels |
| Avoid | "7 ways…", "The truth about…", "Nobody talks about…", "This changed my life" | Overused; clashes with the brand |

Subtitles do the explaining: one sentence that states the tension and promises evidence.
