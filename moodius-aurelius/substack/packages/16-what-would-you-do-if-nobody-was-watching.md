## 6. Three Substack Notes (aim 250–300 characters; embed the post, don't link out)

1. In 2006, a picture of eyes above a coffee-room honesty box nearly tripled what people paid for milk. It became one of the most cited findings in behavioural science. Then two meta-analyses of dozens of experiments found eye images didn't reliably make people more generous. The honest answer is: sometimes, a little. *(316 chars)*
2. Plato's Glaucon bet that anyone with a ring of invisibility would stop behaving well. That's one reading of 'what would you do if nobody was watching?' The other: what would you finally do? The first tests your character. The second tests your courage. *(252 chars)*
3. Most of people's lasting regrets are about hopes they didn't pursue, not duties they missed. Those hopes are often the choices most shaped by imagined judgement. This week: take one decision and ask what you'd choose if nobody would ever know. *(243 chars)*
