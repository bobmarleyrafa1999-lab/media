## 6. Three Substack Notes (aim 250–300 characters; embed the post, don't link out)

1. People in a 2014 study were left alone with their thoughts for 15 minutes and a button that gave a mild shock. Most men pressed it. The headline was 'people would rather be shocked than think'. The better reading: given no direction, many of us don't know what to do with our own minds. That's practice, not fate. *(313 chars)*
2. Pascal, in the 1600s: all of human unhappiness comes from not being able to sit quietly in a room. He wrote that without a phone in sight. Restlessness isn't a smartphone invention. The phone is just the most efficient way we've found to feed it. *(246 chars)*
3. One finding worth knowing: in studies of 15-minute device-free solitude, people who chose what to think about got more of the calming effect. Not emptying your mind. Pointing it somewhere. *(188 chars)*
