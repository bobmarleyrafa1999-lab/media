## 6. Three Substack Notes (aim 250–300 characters; embed the post, don't link out)

1. In 47 experiments that successfully changed what people intended to do, what they actually did moved about half as far. That gap is where most of our repeated mistakes live. Understanding is real. It just doesn't travel into the moment on its own. This week's essay is about the bridge. *(286 chars)*
2. Aristotle on why we repeat what we know is wrong: you can possess knowledge without having it awake when you act, like someone asleep who still "knows" things. Two thousand years later, habit research says something close to the same. *(234 chars)*
3. Stop asking "why do I do this?" for a month. Ask "what sets it off?" Where were you, who was there, what time was it, what did your body do first? The cause is interesting. The cue is the part you can plan for. *(210 chars)*
