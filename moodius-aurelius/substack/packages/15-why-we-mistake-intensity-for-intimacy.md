## 6. Three Substack Notes (aim 250–300 characters; embed the post, don't link out)

1. Fromm warned that people take the intensity of an infatuation as proof of how much they love, when it may only show how lonely they were before. Harsh, and not always true. But a useful question: is this feeling about them, or about how much I needed someone? *(259 chars)*
2. In a 1997 study, strangers who took turns asking gradually more personal questions ended up feeling much closer than pairs making small talk. The ingredient wasn't uncertainty or drama. It was two people revealing a little more, in turn, and responding. *(253 chars)*
3. If calm feels like boredom to you, it may be that your nervous system is used to watching for the next withdrawal and has nothing to do. Safety can feel flat at first. Give it longer than you think. *(198 chars)*
