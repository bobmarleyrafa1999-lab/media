We imagine character is decided in big moments. Aristotle thought it was laid down in small, repeated ones: we become builders by building, and just by doing just things.

Most of those repetitions happen when nobody's watching. That's not time off from who you are. It's where you're being made.

What's one small thing you could do tonight and tell nobody?

Source: Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II (paraphrased). Full essay on the Substack, link in bio.
