Men tend to get two scripts for anger: let it out, or keep it in.

In a 2002 experiment with 600 students, those who hit a punching bag while thinking about the person who'd insulted them ended up angrier and more aggressive than people who sat quietly. And habitual suppression has its own costs.

The third option is slower: name it, then ask what it's protecting.

Which script were you given?

Evidence: Bushman (2002); Gross & John (2003). Full essay on the Substack, link in bio.
