Instagram blocks automated reading of its pages (robots.txt plus a proxy block here), so the account numbers come from Social Blade's public profile summaries, read on 13 Oct 2026. Strategy details come from published case studies and interviews (links at the bottom). "Likes per 100 followers" is average likes divided by followers. It is the fairest way to compare a 3M page with a 13k page. Social Blade averages recent posts and only counts likes, not views or shares, so treat it as a direction, not a precise figure.

What jumps out:
- **Size doesn't buy attention.** Daily Stoic has 7x the followers of The Everyday Stoic but gets fewer likes per post. The big quote pages (@stoicsquotes, @stoicism.philosophy) post into followers who no longer react.

- **Comments are near zero everywhere.** Paths of Stoicism gets 3 comments per post on 1M followers. Nobody in this niche is building conversation, which is the opening for our first-comment questions.

## 2. Page by page: how each one gives information to the public

### @dailystoic: the media company
- **Format:** a mix of Ryan Holiday talking-head clips, quote cards, list carousels and pop-culture memes (e.g. an Office scene). Its single best recent post on Holiday's account was a "5 Stoic all-time one-liners" list, with 7.55M impressions (Socialpruf).
- **Production:** a team (editor, social media person, researcher). One 20-30 minute video gets cut into many reels and turned into articles. They publish daily across about 8 channels.
- **Funnel:** everything points to a free ~500-word daily email (320k+ subscribers, ~40% open rate). The email sells books, $49-499 courses, a paid membership, merch and their bookshop. Holiday stopped paying for ads and put that money into content.
- **What separates it:** a real face and authority (a bestselling author) plus an owned email list. It is a business with Instagram as the shop window, not an Instagram page.
- **Weak spot:** engagement per follower is the lowest in the set, and its YouTube is stalling (1.6M subscribers but only ~28k views in a week).

### @pathsofstoicism: borrowed emotion
- **Format:** anime characters (Ippo, Guts, Goku, Vegeta) paired with one short Stoic line about discipline, introspection or gratitude, e.g. "Protect yourself from your own thoughts."
- **What separates it:** it rides fandoms. Anime fans share it because of the character, then absorb the idea. It aims squarely at young men.
- **Weak spot:** 3 comments per post on 1M followers. People scroll past it rather than talk about it, and it leans on imagery it doesn't own.

### @theeverydaystoic: the friendly translator (best engagement among big pages)
- **Format:** Stoic memes and jokes, quotes, and "Stoic reminders": original advice the owner (William Mulligan) writes from his own week's problems, then condenses. Tagline: "Stoicism for the everyday person." The owner deliberately keeps it less serious and fights gatekeeping.
- **What he says works:** "The feedback I got from posting Stoic themed memes and jokes was incredible." He also puts community first, asking followers to discuss with each other.
- **Also on YouTube:** 438k subscribers.
- **What separates it:** it is human and funny in a niche full of solemn marble. That's why it gets 10x Daily Stoic's engagement per follower.

### @stoicsquotes and @stoicism.philosophy: the quote mills
- **Format:** plain quote graphics, high volume.
- **Result:** 0.09-0.16 likes per 100 followers. This is what happens when every post looks like every other page's post. Their followers stay, but Instagram stops showing them the posts.
- **Lesson:** a quote alone is not content anymore.

### @stoicismdaily (as described in the press, ~122k) and @stoicism.daily (13k, rising)
- **Format:** minimal, polished quotes on paper-texture backgrounds, easy to read and share.
- @stoicism.daily's 7.5 likes per 100 followers suggests posts reaching beyond its followers. I couldn't open its posts (Instagram blocks it), so the exact reason is unconfirmed. It's the first page to add to Metricool Competitors so we can see its top reels.

## 3. What actually separates the winners
1. **A point of view, not a quote.** Winners say something about the quote: Holiday's take, Mulligan's weekly reminder, Einzelgänger's personal angle. The quote mills don't, and they're dying.
2. **A recognisable "who".** A face (Holiday), a character universe (anime) or a voice (Everyday Stoic's humour). Anonymous marble is the default, and the default gets ignored.
3. **Modern problems first, philosophy second.** The best performers open with the reader's problem (anxiety, comparison, discipline, social media) and bring the Stoic in second.
4. **Lists and one-liners travel.** "5 Stoic all-time one-liners" was Holiday's biggest post of the year (7.55M impressions).
5. **An owned destination.** Daily Stoic turns followers into email subscribers, then customers. Pages without one (most quote pages) have nothing to sell when reach drops.
6. **Nobody does conversation.** Comments are near zero across the niche, so a page that actually talks to its followers stands out.

## 4. What this means for Moodius Aurelius
- **Keep:** reels over images, problem-first hooks, list formats, book and chapter on screen, first-comment questions (no one else does them).
- **Add a "who":** give the page a character. Option A: Surafel's voice reading the reels (the plan already asks for this). Option B: a consistent persona, "Moodius", who speaks in first person ("My rule for today:"). Either beats anonymous marble.
- **Add humour 1 in 7 posts:** a Stoic meme or "Marcus Aurelius reacting to modern life" format, after The Everyday Stoic's lesson.
- **One "5 one-liners" list carousel a week,** modelled on Holiday's top post format, written in our own words.
- **Start the owned funnel now:** link-in-bio to a free "7 Stoic rules" PDF that collects emails. That becomes the route to the €7-12 "30-Day Stoic Journal" in the growth plan.
- **Don't copy:** anime imagery (not ours, risky with Instagram's originality rules) or reposted celebrity clips (no longer recommended since 30 Apr 2026).
- **Track live:** add @stoicism.daily, @theeverydaystoic, @dailystoic, @pathsofstoicism and @stoicsquotes to Metricool Competitors. Then the Friday routine reads their top reels each week.

What it says:
1. **Relational, confrontational hooks win.** The three best 3-second view rates (44-48%) were "insult", "argument" and "everyone like you". Numbered-list reels ("3 rules", "5 things") had the lowest hold (25-28%) despite the list-format advice from the top pages. Lists work as carousels, not as 18-second reels.
2. **The 10:00 slot is dead for this account.** Both 10:00 reels got 12-26 views against 115-143 at 12:00 and 18:00. Metricool's generic best-time chart still ranks 10:00 first, but our own data doesn't. Week 3 moves the third slot to 15:00 as a test.
3. **The cap.** Metricool's free plan stops at 20 published posts a month; it ran out on 15 Oct, so Thu-Sun of Week 2 did not publish. Competitors are still not added in Metricool (Analytics > Instagram > Competitors), so the competitor metrics return no rows.

Week 3 plan: 19 reels with "you" hooks and one idea each, 1 meme, 1 carousel ("5 ancient lines for when life gets heavy"), 14 stories, all written in the Moodius voice around the mission (purpose, suffering, amor fati, virtue, beauty). Specs in the repo under moodius-aurelius/week3/.
