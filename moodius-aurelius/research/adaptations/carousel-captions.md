## CA01 · Five sentences instead of "I'm fine" (after @dailystoic's list format)
"I'm fine" ends the conversation, including the one in your own head. These five keep it open a little.

Number 3 comes straight from Epictetus: some things are up to us, most aren't. The rest are ours.

Which one do you need this week? Save it, and send it to the friend who's always "good".

Sources: Moodius one-liners; no. 3 after Epictetus, Enchiridion 1 (tr. George Long).
#stoicism #mentalhealth #selftalk #emotionalintelligence #quiettruths
First comment: Which number are you on today?

## CA02 · Boundary or wall (after Nedra Glover Tawwab)
Both keep people out. Only one leaves a door.

A boundary is a sentence about what you'll do. A wall is silence and hoping they guess. Epictetus would put it simply: your reply is in your power, their behaviour isn't.

Which of the three on slide 2 do you do most?

Quote: Epictetus, Enchiridion 1 (tr. George Long). Reflection: Moodius.
#boundaries #stoicism #relationships #emotionalintelligence #selfrespect
First comment: Write the boundary you need to say this week, in one sentence.

## CA04 · Why you feel behind (after Dr Julie Smith)
Festinger's idea, from 1954: we size ourselves up by looking at other people, especially when we're unsure of ourselves. The feed turned that into a full-time job.

Two steps inside. The first is the one people skip: compare yourself with you, a year ago.

What can you do now that you couldn't last October?

Evidence: psychology theory (Festinger, 1954), in our words. Reflection: Moodius.
#comparison #mentalhealth #stoicism #selfworth #socialmedia
First comment: One thing you can do now that you couldn't a year ago.

## CA05 · Myth: a real Stoic doesn't cry (after Ryan Holiday's myth-busting talks)
"Stoic" in English means a face that shows nothing. Seneca, writing to a friend who'd lost someone, says the opposite: let the eyes be wet, just don't drown.

Feeling it and functioning aren't opposites.

Who taught you that holding it in was strength?

Source: Seneca, Letters 63, Moodius paraphrase (Gummere's translation is the reference). Reflection: Moodius.
#stoicism #grief #seneca #mensmentalhealth #emotionalintelligence
First comment: When did you last let it out?
