# The Moodius sound (21 Oct 2026)

**In one line:** a quiet room at night, a felt piano with the damper half down, low strings that breathe in rather than swell, and one small three-note figure that sounds like a thought being finished.

Reference points: A24 scores that let a scene sit (sparse piano, long room tone, harmony that doesn't resolve on cue), editorial campaigns where the music sits under the image rather than selling it, and understated luxury with nothing shiny about it.

## The five constants (every track, original or licensed)
1. **Felt piano or a single sustained instrument leads.** No choir, no taiko, no risers, no "epic" orchestra.
2. **A floor of room tone and tape hiss** (about −60 dBFS) under every Moodius original, so silence never sounds digital.
3. **The turn.** The harmony moves and a low octave enters exactly on the reel's statement card, the moment the idea turns. The mixer does this automatically.
4. **The motif.** D, A, F (root, fifth, minor third above) on felt piano, played under the CTA. It's the sound logo, and over time it's how people will recognise Moodius before they read the name. A 3 s sting version: `compose.py sting`.
5. **One loudness.** Every export is −14 LUFS integrated, −1.5 dBTP. Music under a voice sits about 12 dB below it, and quote cards get a −3 dB dip so the reading isn't fighting the bed.

## What we never use
Motivational "rise" tracks, trailer hits and drops, trap hi-hats under philosophy, lo-fi hip-hop study beats (overused in this niche), stock "inspiring corporate" piano, trending audio on business reels, and AI-generated tracks from prompt tools (their licences change and the sound is easy to spot).

## Honest notes on quality
- **The originals are synthesised from scratch in Python**: no samples, no models. That's what makes them owned outright and lets them be re-composed to each reel's cuts. They're restrained and consistent, but a synthesised piano is not a recorded Steinway. Listen to the sample reel before deciding how much of the feed they should carry.
- **The 15 curated tracks** (Scott Buckley, Chris Zabriskie, Kevin MacLeod, Musopen recordings) are real recordings under CC BY 4.0 or public domain. Their licences were checked at artist level by web search. Each one's own track page still needs a look at download, and the `verification` column says exactly what's left to confirm.
- **Instagram's in-app music** (Meta Sound Collection on business accounts) is cleared only for use on Meta platforms. It can be added in the app at upload, but it can't go through this pipeline or appear in exported files used elsewhere.
