# Response bank for story replies and DMs

How to use it: pick the closest line, change at least one word so it fits the person, send. Every reply should (1) show you read their message, (2) add one small thing, (3) end with a short follow-up question when it's natural. Keep it under 40 words. Reply within a day where you can: replies tell Instagram you two are close, and that puts your stories first in their bar.

Never: copy-paste the identical line to many people in one night; argue in DMs; promise advice you can't give; send links to the shop in reply to a personal message.

The comment agent (community-agent/) stays draft-only with DMs off. These are for you to send by hand.

## 1. Quiz answers

**They got it right**
- "Correct. You're annoyingly good at this. Did you know it, or was it a lucky guess?"
- "Yes! Most people picked the other one. What gave it away?"
- "Spot on. I got this one wrong the first time I read it."

**They got it wrong**
- "Most people guessed that too, which is sort of the point. The answer's at 9. Want a hint?"
- "Wrong, but in very good company. What made that one feel right?"
- "Nope, but that's the one I'd have picked too. Tomorrow's answer might annoy you."

**They argue with the answer**
- "Fair challenge. The source is [study]. It's one study, so you could be right in your case. What have you seen?"
- "I might have simplified it too much for a story. What's the bit that doesn't match your experience?"

## 2. Polls and dilemmas

- "Interesting, you're with the minority tonight. What tipped it for you?"
- "Same as most people. Has it ever gone the other way for you?"
- "That's the one I'd pick on a good day. On a bad day I'm not so sure. You?"

## 3. Someone shares something personal

- "Thank you for telling me that. It's not a small thing to say. How are you doing with it now?"
- "That sounds like it cost you something. I'm glad you're still here to tell it. What helped?"
- "I read that twice. Thank you. Is there anything that would make this week a bit lighter?"
- "That's honest, and honest is rare. I don't have a clever answer, just: I hear you."

## 4. Someone is struggling (possible crisis)

Don't debate, don't share, don't try to be their therapist. Reply the same day, warmly and plainly:

- UK: "I'm really glad you told me. I'm not a professional, but you deserve to talk to someone who is, right now. Samaritans are free, any time, on 116 123 (or jo@samaritans.org). If you're in danger, call 999. Will you reach out to them?"
- Spain: "…Línea 024 is free, 24 hours, for anyone having thoughts of suicide. If you're in danger, 112."
- Elsewhere: "…your local emergency number, or findahelpline.com lists free lines by country."
Then check in once the next day: "Thinking of you. How are you today?"

## 5. Asking for advice

- "I'm not a therapist, just someone who reads too much philosophy. What I'd try: [one small step]. What have you tried so far?"
- "Honestly, I don't know your situation well enough. What would you tell a friend in the same spot?"
- "Two thoughts, take or leave: [1], [2]. Which feels closer?"

## 6. Asking for a source

- "Here it is: [author, journal, year]. It's [small/big] study, so treat it as a lead, not a law."
- "Good question. It's a paraphrase of [book, section]. The free translation is on Standard Ebooks if you want the full passage."
- "I got that one from [source]. If you find a better one, send it, I'll correct the post."

## 7. They say a fact is wrong

- "Thank you, checking now." Then, if they're right: "You're right. I've corrected it and I'll say so in stories. Thanks for keeping me honest."
- If the source holds: "I checked again: [source] says [x]. Happy to be shown otherwise if you've got something newer."

## 8. Disagreement with a Moodius take

- "Good, I hoped someone would push back. What's the strongest version of your view?"
- "You might be right. Where I'd hesitate is [x]. Does that change anything for you?"
- "I like this. Can I quote your argument anonymously in tomorrow's 'argument against' story?" (Only share if they say yes.)

## 9. Bait, red-pill or rage replies

- First, decide if it's a person or a troll. A real person gets: "I see it differently. Moodius is for everyone, so I won't frame it as men vs women. What's the bit underneath that's bothering you?"
- A troll gets nothing. Don't reply, restrict the account if it repeats (Restrict hides their comments without telling them).
- Never debate politics, religion or gender wars in DMs. "That's not what this page is for, but thanks for reading."

## 10. Compliments and thanks

- "That genuinely made my day. Which post was it that landed?"
- "Thank you. Who else would like it? (Send it to them, it helps more than you think.)"
- "Thank you. I write these for people like you, so it's nice to know you exist."

## 11. "Who are you?" / "Show your face"

- "Just someone who likes old books and new research. The page is about the ideas, not me. What brought you here?"
- "I keep it anonymous so the ideas do the talking. You're not missing much, promise."

## 12. Book replies

- "Adding it to my list. What's one idea from it you still use?"
- "I haven't read that one yet. Would you give it to a friend having a hard year?"
- (Only if you've read it) "Loved that one. Which chapter got you?"

## 13. Kindness stories

- "That's lovely. Did you ever get to thank them? Could I share it anonymously tomorrow?"
- "This is why I asked. Thank you. Have you ever passed it on?"

## 14. Add Yours and 'you pick' votes

- "Thanks for joining in. Yours made me smile."
- "Vote counted. If history wins, any period you'd like?"

## 15. Collaboration, promo and spam DMs

- Promo/shoutout offers: no reply needed. Don't click links.
- Genuine collaboration from a known page: "Thanks, I'm keeping things small for now. Could you email hello@moodius.co.uk?" (No commitments in DMs; anything paid needs your OK and the OBI declaration.)

## 16. One-word replies (most of them)

- "Good answer. Why that one?"
- "Ha, same."
- "Noted. Tomorrow's answer might surprise you."
- A heart reaction is fine for the rest. Not every reply needs words.

## Common discussion themes and how Moodius answers

| Theme | Moodius line | Follow-up question |
|---|---|---|
| Overthinking | "Thinking is fine. Rehearsing the same scene 40 times is a rerun, not a review." | "What's the scene on repeat?" |
| Loneliness | "It's more common than people admit, and it's not a character flaw." | "Who's one person you could message tonight, low stakes?" |
| Anger | "Anger is information with bad manners." | "What was it protecting?" |
| Discipline | "Discipline is mostly remembering why, at the moment you'd rather not." | "What's your why this week?" |
| Comparison | "You're comparing your Tuesday to someone's highlight reel. Rude to your Tuesday." | "What's good about your Tuesday?" |
| Stoicism = no feelings | "Stoics felt everything. They just didn't let every feeling drive." | "Which feeling would you like to let drive less?" |
| Grief | "There's no correct speed." | "What do you miss most?" (Only if they've opened up first.) |
| Burnout | "Rest before you're empty, not after." | "What would one easier day look like?" |
