# 50 Story concepts (+3 recurring formats)

Each concept: hook, layout (template), the sticker to add by hand, the reply prompt for the scheduled version, a discussion question, the follow-up story, an optional CTA and the source. The 15 strongest are written out frame by frame in 03-sequences.md.

Moodius takes are drafts in the page's voice: change anything that isn't true for you.

## An uncomfortable question

**C25 · The Human Mind**: Who do you envy? Don't answer out loud. Just notice how fast a name came.

- Layout: T01 hook
- Sticker (by hand): Question box: 'What does your envy want?' (answer anonymously)
- Scheduled version: Reply with what they have, not who they are.
- Discussion question: If envy is a map, where is it pointing?
- Follow-up story: Moodius take: envy is rude but useful. It points at something you want and haven't admitted. The question is whether you want their life or one thing in it.
- Source: Opinion, labelled as Moodius

- Source: Moodius paraphrase of Epictetus, Enchiridion 1

## What psychology actually says

**C01 · The Human Mind**: You spilled coffee on your shirt before a meeting. How many people noticed?

- Layout: T08 quiz
- Sticker (by hand): Quiz: A about half / B about a quarter / C nearly everyone (answer B)
- Scheduled version: Reply A, B or C.
- Discussion question: What's the last thing you were sure everyone noticed?
- Follow-up story: Answer card: students made to wear an embarrassing T-shirt guessed about half the room noticed. About a quarter did. Moodius: the spotlight is mostly in your head, which is good news and mildly insulting.
- CTA: Send this to the friend who still talks about the thing from 2019.
- Source: Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, J. Personality and Social Psychology 78(2), 2000

**C02 · The Good Life**: You just met someone new and chatted for five minutes. Did they like you more or less than you think?

- Layout: T02 two sides
- Sticker (by hand): Poll: More than I think / Less than I think
- Scheduled version: Reply 'more' or 'less'.
- Discussion question: Who did you misjudge on a first meeting?
- Follow-up story: Researchers call it the liking gap: after first conversations, people kept underestimating how much the other person liked them. Moodius: you're probably more liked than your 1am brain allows.
- CTA: Send this to someone who replays every conversation.
- Source: Boothby, Clark, Corcoran & Bargh, Psychological Science 29(11), 2018

**C03 · The Good Life**: You think a compliment will feel awkward. The other person thinks...?

- Layout: T03 psychology says
- Sticker (by hand): Slider 😬 → 😊: how good does a stranger feel after a sincere compliment?
- Scheduled version: Reply with a number 1 to 10.
- Discussion question: Who deserves a compliment you haven't said out loud?
- Follow-up story: People giving compliments underestimated how good they made others feel and overestimated how awkward it would be. Moodius: the awkwardness is a toll you pay once.
- CTA: Send one compliment today. Reply 'sent' if you did.
- Source: Boothby & Bohns, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 47(5), 2021

**C04 · The Good Life**: Talk to the stranger on the train, or keep your headphones in?

- Layout: T02 two sides
- Sticker (by hand): Poll: Talk / Headphones
- Scheduled version: Reply 'talk' or 'headphones'.
- Discussion question: When did a conversation with a stranger surprise you?
- Follow-up story: Commuters asked to talk to a stranger reported a more pleasant journey than those told to keep to themselves, though most predicted the opposite. Moodius: nobody is asking you to befriend the whole carriage. One sentence.
- Source: Epley & Schroeder, J. Experimental Psychology: General 143(5), 2014

**C05 · The Good Life**: Ordering coffee: efficient, or one real sentence with the barista?

**C07 · The Human Mind**: Angry. Hurt. Fine. How many words do you actually use for feelings?

- Layout: T09 slider
- Sticker (by hand): Slider 🫥 → 🎨: how precisely can you name what you feel?
- Scheduled version: Reply with today's feeling in one word. 'Fine' not allowed.
- Discussion question: What's a feeling you've never had a word for?
- Follow-up story: People who tell their emotions apart in fine detail tend to cope better under stress. Psychologists call it emotional granularity. Moodius: 'annoyed, slightly jealous, mostly tired' is a better sentence than 'fine'.
- Source: Kashdan, Barrett & McKnight, Current Directions in Psychological Science 24(1), 2015

**C08 · The Human Mind**: Saying 'I'm angry' out loud. Does it make it worse or better?

- Scheduled version: Reply A, B or C.
- Discussion question: What's the feeling you avoid naming?
- Follow-up story: In a brain-scan study, putting a feeling into words lowered the amygdala's response compared with other tasks. Name it to tame it, roughly. Small lab study, not a cure.
- Source: Lieberman et al., Psychological Science 18(5), 2007

**C09 · The Human Mind**: Before something scary: 'calm down' or 'I'm excited'?
