# Comment reply playbook for @moodiusaurelius

The voice: a calm older friend who has read Marcus, Seneca and Epictetus. Warm, short, never preachy. Every reply does one of three things: it gives a line of wisdom, it asks a follow-up question (more replies mean more reach), or it thanks someone by name.

## The rules
1. Reply within the first 60 minutes after a post goes live. Early replies tell Instagram the post is a conversation.
2. Keep replies to 1-3 lines. End about half of them with a question.
3. Use the person's words back to them. Don't use generic "Thanks! 🙏".
4. Never argue. Answer trolls with one calm Stoic line, or don't answer at all.
5. Pin the best comment on each post.
6. Turn "Send this to someone" commenters into DMs. A short thank-you DM builds loyalty.

## Reply bank (copy, then tweak a word or two so no two replies are identical)

**Someone shares a struggle** ("I'm going through a lot right now")
- "Marcus wrote, 'The mind adapts and converts to its own purposes the obstacle.' You're already doing the work by naming it. What's one small thing within your control today?"
- "Seneca said we suffer more in imagination than in reality. Be gentle with yourself this week. I'm glad you're here."
- "Hard seasons are training, not punishment. Keep going, one honest day at a time."

**Someone shares an answer to the question in the caption**
- "That's a strong answer. Epictetus would say it isn't the thing, it's the judgement about the thing. How did you work that out?"
- "'Very little is needed to make a happy life.' You just proved it. Thank you for sharing."
- "Love this. Write it down tonight in your evening review, because it'll mean more in a month."

**Praise** ("This is so true", "Needed this", 🔥)
- "Glad it found you at the right time. Who else needs to see it?"
- "Then it's done its job. Come back tomorrow, there's more."
- "Thank you, [name]. Which line hit hardest?"

**A question about Stoicism** ("Where do I start?")
- "Start with Meditations, the Gregory Hays translation. Read one page a morning, not one book a week."
- "Begin with the evening review: three questions, five minutes, every night. That's Stoicism as practice, not theory."
- "Epictetus' Enchiridion is short enough to read in an afternoon and deep enough to reread for life."

**Disagreement or debate** ("Stoicism is just suppressing emotions")
- "A fair challenge. The Stoics didn't kill emotion, they questioned it. Feel it fully, then ask whether it's telling you the truth."
- "Good point to raise. Marcus cried, grieved and loved. The goal was to not be ruled by those feelings, not to stop having them."

**Trolls and insults**
- "'The best revenge is not to be like your enemy.' Have a good day, friend."
- Or no reply. Hide the comment if it's abusive.

**Spam or bots** ("DM us for promo")
- Delete it, don't reply.

**Comments in other languages**
- Reply in their language if you can, with the same wisdom-plus-question shape.

## Daily routine (about 10 minutes)
- 15-60 minutes after each post: reply to every comment, pin the best one.
- Once in the evening: reply to anything new, and DM 3 people who shared the post.
- Once a week: comment thoughtfully on 10 posts from bigger Stoic accounts (2-3 lines each, adding something). This is the cheapest growth there is.

## What's already automated
Each scheduled post now goes out with its own first comment: a second quote plus a question, to seed the conversation.
