## Route 1: your own journal + free guide + newsletter

- Why first: you keep about £10.77 of each £14 journal after VAT and fees (pricing model, 22 Oct). One sale a month covers the shop's fixed costs. No brand, no exclusivity, no one else's claims to vouch for.
- What it needs from you: OBI approved, Stripe live, one test purchase, "go live". Then the funnel already built (free guide → 5 welcome emails → journal) switches on.
- On Instagram: at most 1 shop post per 8 feed posts, never inside a meme. Self-promotion is still advertising under UK guidance, so the post says "Ad" or "Our own product" at the start.

## Route 2: Bookshop.org UK affiliate on book picks

- Terms (Awin merchant 62675, checked 27 Oct): 10% commission on sales, plus a matching 10% to independent bookshops; 30-day attribution. Payment timing isn't stated on the merchant page, so it goes on the approval card as "to confirm with Awin".
- Honest scale: a £10 book earns £1. This is a small, on-brand trickle that grows with the Reading lane. Not a living.
- How it would look: a Reading page on moodius.co.uk listing books with "Affiliate links: we earn 10% if you buy, at no extra cost to you". Instagram posts that point to it start with "Ad · affiliate link in bio".
- Rule: we only describe what a book argues (with sources). "I loved it" only when it's true and you wrote it.
- Anonymity: Awin needs real identity and bank details. They're private to Awin, but read the account-separation guide first.

## Disclosure rules we'll follow (UK)

From the ASA/CMA influencer guidance as summarised by [RPC, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/) and [Instagram's branded content rules](https://help.instagram.com/android-app/1109894795810258):
- Label anything paid, gifted, loaned or affiliate, and your own products: "Ad" at the very start of the caption and on the image. "Collab", "spon" or a brand tag alone aren't enough.
- Brand deals also use Instagram's **Paid partnership** label (Instagram requires it). The label alone may not be enough, so we add "Ad" too.
- The CMA can now fine serious breaches directly (DMCC Act 2024).
- No claims we can't back up: no health, results or income claims for any product.
- Re-check the ASA guidance itself before the first commercial post (the summary above is a law-firm snapshot; asa.org.uk wasn't fetched from here).
