## 2. A third-party breakdown of his storytelling (OBSERVED by someone else)

2. **Mission as a question**: "is it possible to win one of the big prizes at an arcade?"
3. **Stakes vs conventional wisdom**: "Conventional wisdom says the house always wins…"
4. **Self-deprecating joke**: "I know I sound like a gambling addict."
5. **Progress number**: "12,000 tickets down, but we still need 60,000."
6. **Cliffhanger**: "We weren't ready for what was about to happen on day two."
7. **Twist**, with the music turning from upbeat to sombre.
8. **Callback ending**: back to "The house always wins", plus a dry closing joke.

## 4. What we infer (INFERRED)

- I1. **The engine is the serial.** A day counter plus a finish line plus a progress number gives every episode a built-in reason to come back, and the finale cashes it in. Spikes come from story arcs with stakes, not from tips.
- I2. **Personal proof is his trust device.** He is the experiment. Moodius cannot show a face, so the proof has to come from somewhere else: a source (a book, a historical record) or the followers themselves (counted replies).
- I3. **His sell is invisible in the reel.** The reel is story; the bio link does the selling, and a free live event with bonuses does the converting.
- I5. **His two hooks are both promises of a test.** "Is it possible?" sets up a challenge with an unknown ending; "Here's how to" + objection pre-empts the viewer's doubt. Both suit an anonymous page because neither needs a face, only a question and an answer.
- I4. **Relatable sketches came first.** "That one friend who…" is the same move as our "send this to the friend who…" share line, which our own data already rewards.

Nothing here promises a viral result. His numbers come from a face-led, personality account. Ours is anonymous and small (59 followers on 25 Oct), so every format below is a test with a stop rule.
