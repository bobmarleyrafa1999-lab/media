The short version

  • StatedHis only two hooks: an "Is it possible?" challenge, and "Here's how to…" plus the viewer's objection. His storytelling rules: one clear message, three acts, curiosity loops, sound design.
  • StatedNo pitching inside the video: "the instant viewers feel like they're getting pitched… they're clicking off." The bio link and a free class do the selling.
  • ObservedHis 4 biggest TikToks in our sample are day-counter series and finales ("day 340 of road to $100k a month", 756K views). But series posts have a lower median than his other posts (14,100 vs 22,000). Series make spikes, not a steadier baseline.
  • InferredHis trust device is himself as the experiment. Moodius can't do that, so the proof comes from sources and from followers' counted replies.

His formulas, faceless

F0

His two hooks

"Is it possible to read a whole Stoic handbook in 7 days?" and "Here's how to calm a 2am spiral. No, not by thinking positive."

F1

Counter serial

"Day 1 of 7." The follower is the experiment; day 2 opens with the real number of people who replied DONE. Same book, same desk, a growing tally. Finale is a carousel.

F2

Story arc

Mission, stakes, twist, callback, told about a historical person on text cards and public-domain engravings.

F3

Result first

Open on the outcome from the historical record, never a personal "this changed my life".

F4

Pep talk vs the old version

His "slop vs viral" split, as a generic slogan beside a sourced line. Ends "A or B?"

F5

That one friend

His early sketch format as a chat mock-up, then a sourced line. Ends "Send this to that friend."

F6

Countdown to a free thing

His pre-launch countdowns, used only if you say yes to the read-along. Every day still gives one useful line.

Not copied: income counters, "generational wealth", posting 30 times a day, anything face-to-camera.
