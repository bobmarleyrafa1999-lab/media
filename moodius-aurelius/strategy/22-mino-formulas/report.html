The short version

  • StatedHis only two hooks: an "Is it possible?" challenge, and "Here's how to…" plus the viewer's objection. His storytelling rules: one clear message, three acts, curiosity loops, sound design.
  • StatedNo pitching inside the video: "the instant viewers feel like they're getting pitched… they're clicking off." The bio link and a free class do the selling.
  • ObservedHis 4 biggest TikToks in our sample are day-counter series and finales ("day 340 of road to $100k a month", 756K views). But series posts have a lower median than his other posts (14,100 vs 22,000). Series make spikes, not a steadier baseline.
  • InferredHis trust device is himself as the experiment. Moodius can't do that, so the proof comes from sources and from followers' counted replies.

What changed after your yes

  • Mino's 15 editing rules, applied. Every card is fully on screen from its first frame (no fades). No line ends on a weak word like "a" or "the". Nothing important sits in the top bar Instagram covers. The renderer now blocks emoji on screen.
  • New layout. A small orange series label at the top ("A story from Rome", "A or B"), the words in a calm middle band, and a row of progress ticks that fills as the reel plays. The handle sits low, above the caption.
  • Real video behind the words. Candles, Roman ruins filmed 1914-1918, a city at night and cloud timelapses. The video cuts exactly when the words change. All seven clips are public domain, CC0 or CC BY, from Wikimedia Commons. I checked every frame by eye: no faces, no people, no AI footage. Clips that failed (a talking head, a hand, an AI clock, a cartoon) were thrown out.
  • Credit lines. Four clips are CC BY, so their reels carry a one-line credit at the end of the caption. The licence for every clip is recorded in the repo.
