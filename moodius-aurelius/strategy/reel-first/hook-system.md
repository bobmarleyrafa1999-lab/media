# Hook and hold system for Moodius reels (22 Oct 2026)

Every reel is 12-18 s of text cards, no face, no voice. These seven rules adapt documented retention techniques to that format.
A script must pass at least 6 of the 7 before it is rendered. The render gate already enforces reading speed (0.4 s + 1 s per 4.5 words per card).

| # | Rule | What it means in a text reel | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Promise = cover = card 1** | The cover text and the first card are the same words, and card 2 starts delivering on them. No bait. | MrBeast production guide (2024): "The title and thumbnail ... set the expectations for the viewer"; viewers who feel "lied to ... click off". https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/ |
| 2 | **Open a gap the viewer half-knows** | The hook raises one specific question about something familiar (a person, a fight, a habit) and holds the answer back until the second-last card. | Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity" (Psychological Bulletin, 1994): curiosity comes from attention to a gap in what you know; people aren't curious about things they know nothing about. https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/ |
| 3 | **Start mid-scene** | Card 1 is a moment already happening ("You checked their profile again"), never a lesson or a name ("Marcus Aurelius said..."). | Casey Neistat: a story needs a reason to care early, and he admits one film took "five minutes before I ... cared". Danny Duncan credits unstaged, real moments ("I try to always be myself"). Neither has published a hook method; this rule is our reading of their work. https://creatorhub.patreon.com/articles/digital-spaghetti-casey-neistat · https://dailybruin.com/2016/03/28/qa-youtube-prankster-danny-duncan-discusses-filming-future-plans |
| 4 | **Three acts in 15 seconds** | Setup (the moment) → conflict (why it hurts, or the turn) → resolution (the line, the source, one act). | Casey Neistat: "it's a basic three-act narrative. It's setup, conflict, resolution." (Patreon Creator Hub interview) |
| 5 | **Cut mid-sentence** | Split one sentence across two cards so the eye has to follow the cut ("You'd never let a stranger..." / "...decide what you're worth."). At most once per reel. | The "Neistat cut-off": cutting away mid-sentence as the viewer anticipates the point (Sven Pape's breakdown). https://nofilmschool.com/2016/08/7-storytelling-techniques-you-can-learn-filmmaker-youtube-star-casey-neistat |
| 6 | **Re-engage at the midpoint** | Around 7-9 s, change the pattern: an Ember "BUT" or "THE OTHER READING" label, a number, or a question card. | MrBeast guide: "It's important to re-engage the viewer around this time because they could get bored ... and click off." |
| 7 | **Pay off in the hook's own words** | The second-last card answers the gap from card 1, reusing its key word, so the ending reads back into the start and the reel loops. | Neistat on the ending delivering on the opening ("I'm gonna deliver on" it) and on "surprise and delight". |

Three additions from the profile clear-out thread's per-post review (strategy/16-lessons-2026-10-22.md):
- **Card 2 by second 3 must part-pay the hook.** Viewers drop between seconds 3 and 6, so card 2 gives a first real piece of the answer. The full payoff still lands on the second-last card (rule 7).
- **Named pattern "Two branches".** "If it's true... If it's false..." held 16.5 s average watch against 3-6 s for every other reel. Use it in at least 1 reel a week (C formats or R3).
- **Spacing.** Never post two reels within 2 hours of each other, and never more than 2 reels a day. On the one 3-reel day, the first two reached about 20 people each.

Our own data backs rules 2-3 and 6: hooks about another person or a conflict kept a median 43% past 3 s (others 29%), and the one reel with a two-branch turn ("if true... if false...") held 16.5 s against a 4.3 s median. Instagram's head Adam Mosseri is widely reported to name watch time, sends per reach and likes per reach as the main ranking signals; we could not find his exact wording, so treat that as reported, not quoted.

What we don't copy: vlog jump-cuts, faces, pranks, loud music, fake urgency ("WAIT for it"), bait covers. Phase 1 rules (palette, citations, anonymity) stay.

## Angle mix (from 2 Nov, Surafel's direction 22 Oct)

Not everything comes from hurt. Each week's 12 reels aim for: 3+ **appreciation** (noticing what's good: people, time, small things, memory), 3+ **history or psychology** (a real figure, a study, a mechanism), at most 4 **hurt, conflict or other-person** reels (still the best tested hooks, kept but capped), the rest **life and philosophy**. The tracker's `angle` column tags each reel. Appreciation hooks follow the same 7 rules: start mid-scene, open a gap, pay it off ("Yesterday had sixteen waking hours. Which one would you keep?").

## Comment rule (every post)

1. The caption's last line is a question a viewer can answer in 1-3 words or one emoji, about themselves: "Which hour would you keep?", "One word for someone you miss." Never "thoughts?" or "agree?".
2. The account's first comment answers it first, so the thread isn't empty (Metricool firstCommentText).
3. On-screen last card: "Send this to..." on other-person and conflict reels; "Answer below" on appreciation, question and history reels.
4. Every deep-dive's last slide asks "Who should the next deep dive be? Comment a name." The most-named fitting figure is the next arc.
5. Surafel replies by hand to every comment in the first hour. Weekly report adds comments per reach.

## Build-up to a deep dive (one arc every 3 weeks)

Short form stays the default. Every third week, three seed reels build to one researched long post:
- **Seeds:** 3 reels across Thu-Sat at the usual slots, each covering one true, surprising piece of the figure's life or idea. Cover tag "Part 1 of 3". Last card: "Sunday: the full story."
- **Deep dive:** Sunday 12:00, a 10-12 slide carousel: life in 3 slides, 3 ideas with sources, uncertainties labelled, one practice, and the comment question. When the Substack is live, a linked essay goes out the following Thursday.
- **Figures** rotate across history, pedagogy, philosophy, athletics, intellectual life and art. Facts come from a sourced encyclopedia entry or biography. Quotes are used only if public domain; otherwise "Idea: ... · Moodius words".
