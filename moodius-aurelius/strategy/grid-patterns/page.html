Grid patterns and visual tools

Three ways to make the profile grid form a pattern when someone zooms out, built from the real reels and carousels planned for 3 to 8 Nov. Then a colour-coded list of GitHub repos for better visuals. Nothing has been scheduled or changed in Metricool.

Why the grid looks flat today

Instagram fills the grid newest-first, three to a row, and each new post pushes every older one along by one place. So a pattern tied to post order survives every new post. A pattern tied to fixed positions (puzzle grids, a theme per row) breaks the first time a post is late or extra. That's why all three options below are order patterns: a cycle of 2 makes a checkerboard, a cycle of 4 makes diagonals.

The three patterns

Bone at 12:00 Slate at 18:00 Ember rule only

Recommended. The calmest of the three and the easiest to keep: with two posts a day, every 12:00 post is Bone and every 18:00 post is Slate, so the checkerboard holds by itself. It stays inside the current palette and the "one Ember accent" rule.

Ember Bone (12:00 alternate) Slate (every 18:00)

The boldest. Ember and Bone run as diagonal stripes through Slate, so the profile is recognisable at a glance. The catch: a full Ember tile every fourth post stretches the "one Ember accent" rule, and the stripes only read once 12 or more posts are up. Good for a later refresh.

Bone Ink Petrol Verdigris rule

Use this if you pick one of the earthy blue and green logos (A to D). It's the same checkerboard as A, swapped to Ink Petrol with Verdigris rules. Switching later is one word in the tool, so you can start on A now and move to C without breaking the pattern.

Not chosen: puzzle grids and row themes. Each one needs posts to land in exact positions. At 14 posts a week, one missed slot shifts the whole thing. They also make no sense in the Reels feed, where people see one tile at a time.

How covers land in the right place by themselves

I've built a draft tool, tools/grid_patterns.py. It reads the tracker, numbers every post from 2 Nov 12:00 onwards, and gives post n the colour at position n in the cycle. It made every image on this page. It also writes a ledger (strategy/grid-patterns/grid-ledger-checker.csv) listing each post's colour up to 19 Nov.

  1. Covers become designed images, not video frames. Today engine.py grabs a frame from the hook. It would instead call the tool's cover() with the ledger colour. The frame grab stays as a fallback. All text sits inside the middle 1080×1440 area, so the grid crop never cuts a word.
  2. Carousel slide 1 in stills_v2.py takes the same colour (Slate text on Bone). The inner slides stay as they are.
  3. Scheduling sends the cover. Metricool accepts a custom reel cover (videoThumbnailUrl), so the Friday routine attaches it next to the video. The reel itself still plays on Slate.
  4. The pattern corrects itself. The 20:40 daily check marks each post as published in the ledger. If a post fails or an extra one goes out, the colours for the next posts are recalculated before they render. Archiving old posts never moves the newer tiles.
  5. The X5 hook test stays clean. In all three patterns, every 18:00 reel keeps the same dark cover (Slate, or Ink Petrol in C), so cover colour can't skew the 18:00 hook results.

Pins: pin either none or three posts (one full row). Pinning one or two leaves loose tiles above the pattern. Posts already queued to 1 Nov keep their current covers, so the top four rows are fully patterned by about 8 Nov.

Reel text and motion

tkarabela/pysubs2🟢 Use
MIT · last commit 27 Sep 2026 · Python

Writes styled subtitle files with per-word timing and fade or slide effects, which FFmpeg then burns in. That gives word-by-word reveals and moving text without leaving FFmpeg, our fast default. It's the cheapest real upgrade to the reels.

remotion-dev/remotion🟢 Keep as is
Remotion licence (free for teams of 3 or fewer) · last commit 22 Oct 2026

Already prototyped on R001. Same verdict as before: use it only for layouts FFmpeg can't do, such as split screens and captions over footage.

gl-transitions/gl-transitions🟠 Maybe
MIT · last commit 21 Oct 2026

A library of video transitions. Most are flashy and off-brand. One soft dissolve could be useful, and FFmpeg's built-in xfade already does that.

motion-canvas/motion-canvas · midrender/revideo🟠 Maybe
Both MIT · last commits 2 Jul and 15 Jul 2026 · TypeScript

Strong kinetic typography, but they overlap with Remotion. Running a second code-based video tool would add upkeep for little gain.

Zulko/moviepy · mifi/editly🟠 Maybe
Both MIT · last commits 26 Aug 2026 and 20 Feb 2025 (editly has one maintainer and has been quiet for 20 months)

Both build videos from a recipe, which our engine already does. MoviePy is fine for one-off composites. Neither replaces anything we have.

ManimCommunity/manim · remotion-dev/template-tiktok🔴 Skip
MIT; Remotion licence · last commits 20 Oct and 16 Oct 2026

Manim is built for maths animation, which is the wrong look for us. The Remotion captions template makes bouncing TikTok-style captions, and pysubs2 does the same job more quietly in FFmpeg.

Background footage and open images

jjjake/internetarchive🟢 Use
AGPL-3.0 (fine for a tool we run, never bundle it into the shop site) · last commit 14 Oct 2026

Searches and downloads archive.org, including the Prelinger Archives of public-domain mid-century film. That's real, grainy footage of streets, trains and kitchens for slow backgrounds, with no AI stock. Check each item's rights field before using it. Not yet tested from the cloud, so it may need your Mac.

metmuseum/openaccess🟢 Use
CC0 data · last commit 19 Oct 2026

The Met's full catalogue as one file, with a public-domain flag on every object. Filter to the Photographs and Paintings departments only, because statues and marble are off-brand.

WordPress/openverse🟢 Use the API
MIT code · last commit 13 Oct 2026

One search across hundreds of millions of openly licensed images. Use the public API with the licence filter set to CC0 and public domain only. Nothing needs installing.

Smithsonian/OpenAccess🔴 Skip
CC0 · last commit 21 Dec 2021

The data dump hasn't been updated in almost 5 years. The Met and Openverse cover the same ground with current data.
