## 3. What the numbers say about tone

His Instagram export (feed only):

| post | theme | reach | follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rude people, Marcus planned for it (22 Oct) | HURT | 585 | 0 |
| Replay the argument at 2am (23 Oct) | HURT | 473 | 0 |
| Self-trust, not discipline (21 Oct) | STRUGGLE (reframe) | 342 | **5** |
| Evening review (24 Oct, 1 day old) | PRACTICE | 6 | 0 |
| Five quote cards and the meme (7-14 Oct) | mixed | 4-9 | 0 |

Conflict hooks do pull reach (this matches doc 16 and the Words That Hold report: other-person openers held 43% at 3s vs 29%). But the one post that made followers was a reframe, and positive topics have never been tested in a fair format: the only appreciation post was a quote card at 10:00, the format and slot that failed for everything. So "positive doesn't work" is untested, not proven.

The research in doc 17 points the same way: sadness lowers sharing while awe raises it (Berger & Milkman 2012).

**The fix is to keep the tension in the hook and change what it pays off into.** Open on a recognisable scene, a question or a surprising fact; land on kindness, discovery or a practice. Examples:

- "The man who stopped Boston's smallpox was enslaved." (Onesimus, HM014)
- "A 1,600-year-old recipe won a Nobel Prize." (Ge Hong to Tu Youyou, HM001)
- "Your brain treats giving like getting." (KH001)
- "Nobody knows which tree this is." (Timbuktu, HM012)
- "Confucius told his best student: not yet." (PH004)
- "Marcus had a trick for bad moods: other people." (KH005)
